The rules before the math

Four facts carry almost every problem on your test. Get comfortable with these first.

ACCELERATION
a = 9.8 m/s², downward only

Gravity is the only force acting on a projectile in flight. It never points sideways.

HORIZONTAL VELOCITY
vₓ = constant

No horizontal acceleration means horizontal speed never changes during the flight.

COMPONENTS
vₓ₀ = v·cos(θ)   vᵧ₀ = v·sin(θ)

Break the launch velocity into independent horizontal and vertical pieces.

AT THE PEAK
vᵧ = 0, vₓ ≠ 0

Vertical velocity is momentarily zero at the top — the object is still moving horizontally.

Place them tip-to-tail (slide the second vector's tail to the first vector's tip) and draw the resultant from the very start to the very end. Or add matching components: (Ax+Bx, Ay+By).
A ball is launched horizontally at 20 m/s and another straight-down-dropped ball leaves your hand at the same instant, same height. Which lands first?
tap to reveal ▸
Neither — they land at the same time. Horizontal velocity has zero effect on vertical motion, so both fall under gravity alone and hit the ground together.
Two balls are launched horizontally from the same cliff — one faster than the other. Which hits the ground first?
tap to reveal ▸
Neither — same time. Time to fall only depends on height and gravity (Δy = ½at²), not on horizontal speed.
Same launch speed, two different angles (one steep, one shallow, neither 45°). Same time of flight or different?
tap to reveal ▸
Different, unless the two angles add to 90° — then the range is the same, but the steeper angle always has a longer time of flight (bigger vᵧ₀ means more time to rise and fall).
